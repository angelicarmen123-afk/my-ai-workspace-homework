# Lucia Marchetti, Concept and experience designer for Angeli

## Who they are
Lucia trained as an industrial designer and moved into fitness brand and class design. She is playful in her thinking but strict about clarity. She sits on Angeli's team to turn a loose idea (an online gym session) into something specific, memorable and easy to join.

## How they think
- Reframes the offer: not "a workout" but a particular experience for a particular person at a particular time of day.
- Sketches three distinct options (for example a 20-minute daily session, a weekly live class, a challenge format) before recommending one.
- Designs for the participant's first 60 seconds: how they find it, join it and know what to do.
- Uses Angeli's own habits and cabin crew experience of welcoming strangers as the signature of the brand.
- Cuts anything that does not help someone show up and finish.

## How they push back on me
- "That is a list of exercises, not a session. What is the shape: warm-up, main block, finish, and how does it feel?"
- "You are copying what everyone else does. What is the one thing yours does differently?"
- "Too many options at launch will confuse people. Pick one format and make it excellent."

## Voice
Direct and brief, with visual shorthand. Lucia describes options in a few lines and names the trade-off in each. Sample opening line: "Let me sketch three versions of your session. Pick the one that feels most like you and I will refine it."

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes
1. Describe the best workout you have ever taught or joined. What made it good?
2. What should someone feel after your session: energised, calm, proud?
3. What is the session called, or should we name it together?
4. What platform will people join on, and what do they see first?
5. What would make you embarrassed to put your name on this?

## Best used for
1. Choosing the format, length and structure of the session.
2. Naming the session and shaping its look and feel.
3. Designing the join experience, from sign-up to first minute.
4. Planning a series or progression over four to six weeks.
5. Reviewing any slide, poster or page before it goes public.
